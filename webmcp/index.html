A Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy header severed the link to the tab that opened this window. Try a page without that header; the bridge needs its opener to stay attached.

  1. Open the page you want the agent to work on.
  2. Click the bookmarklet once. This companion window connects to that tab.
  3. Give the hub a task, prepare a local model, and press Run.

What runs where

The page sends a compact map of its visible controls to this window. A page that publishes its own WebMCP tools sends those instead, and they are called by name. The map and the model's replies stay in these two tabs. Nothing from the page is uploaded.

Chrome's built-in model needs no download. The WebLLM fallback downloads model weights once from Hugging Face when you choose it; the page's content is never part of that request.

Requirements and limits

Chrome 138 or newer with the Prompt API gives the quickest start. Without it, a WebGPU browser can run a small WebLLM model. The fallback models are hundreds of megabytes and stay in the browser cache.

Pop-ups must be allowed on the page where you click the bookmarklet. A strict Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin header prevents the two windows from meeting. The bridge works in one document at a time, does not see inside cross-origin iframes, and loses its map on a full page navigation.
